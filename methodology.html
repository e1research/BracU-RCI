Research Capacity Index (RCI) Specification

The Research Capacity Index (RCI) is a standardized scalar metric bounded between [0.00, 1.00]. It combines verified bibliometric indices, authorship seniority roles, and semantic LLM alignment to help students identify suitable thesis supervisors and research collaborators.

Formal Formulation

The Master Equation

// RCI Objective Function

RCI = (0.22 · S_prod) + (0.23 · S_impact) + (0.18 · S_lead) + (0.12 · S_collab) + (0.15 · S_momentum) + (0.10 · S_align)

Where every sub-component S_x ∈ [0.00, 1.00], guaranteeing that the terminal index satisfies 0.00 ≤ RCI ≤ 1.00.

Mathematical Dissection

Component Equations & Rationale

01

S_prod — Peer-Reviewed Productivity (Weight: 22%)

w₁ = 0.22
S_prod = 0.70 · [ ln(1 + W_peer) / ln(1 + 70) ] + 0.30 · [ ln(1 + W_total) / ln(1 + 100) ]

Variables: W_peer = Verified peer-reviewed works (indexed journals & refereed proceedings in ORCID); W_total = Total works including preprints, books, and patents.

Why it matters for students: Differentiates between peer-reviewed articles vetted by external reviewers and unrefereed preprints. Logarithmic damping (ln) prevents researchers with 200+ micro-publications from skewing the metric, rewarding steady, substantial publishing volume.
02

S_impact — Global Citation Impact & Venues (Weight: 23%)

w₂ = 0.23
S_impact = 0.70 · min(1, [ ln(1 + C_total) / ln(1 + 2000) ]) + 0.30 · min(1, [ (C_total / max(1, W_total)) / 30 ])

Variables: C_total = Cumulative global citations resolved via DOI metadata; C_total / W_total = Mean citations per work (normalized against a benchmark of 30 citations/paper).

Why it matters for students: High cumulative citations prove that the academic community actively builds upon this researcher's findings. The secondary term (citations-per-paper) protects against "citation inflation" caused by sheer volume without individual paper depth.
03

S_lead — Leadership & Authorship Position (Weight: 18%)

w₃ = 0.18
S_lead = min(1.0, [ (1.00 · N_first) + (1.25 · N_last) ] / max(1, N_first + N_last))

Variables: N_first = First-author publications (hands-on technical lead); N_last = Last-author publications (senior supervisory, laboratory head, or principal investigator).

Why it matters for students: In computing and sciences, the last author is traditionally the senior professor who conceptualized and guided the study, while the first author executed the core experimentation. High last-authorship indicates active mentorship and laboratory capacity to guide thesis students.
04

S_collab — Collaboration Breadth (Weight: 12%)

w₄ = 0.12
S_collab = 1.00 if 2.0 ≤ μ_authors ≤ 7.0 0.80 if 7.0 < μ_authors ≤ 12.0 0.70 if 1.0 ≤ μ_authors < 2.0 (Solo or isolated author) 0.55 if μ_authors > 12.0 (Hyper-authorship penalty)

Variables: μ_authors = Average number of co-authors per publication across evaluated papers.

Why it matters for students: Students thrive under mentors who collaborate within realistic, close-knit research groups (2 to 7 co-authors). The metric penalizes hyper-authorship consortium papers (50+ authors where the researcher's individual contribution is diluted) as well as isolated solo publishing.
05

S_momentum — 3-Year Publication Recency (Weight: 15%)

w₅ = 0.15
S_momentum = min(1.0, W_recent_3yr / 5)

Variables: W_recent_3yr = Number of verified publications produced within the preceding 36 months (Current Year − 3 to Present).

Why it matters for students: Protects students from selecting "dormant" supervisors who were prolific a decade ago but no longer maintain an active lab or grant funding. A score of 1.0 is awarded for producing at least 5 publications in the past 3 years.
06

S_align — Societal, Humanitarian & Frontier Relevance (Weight: 10%)

w₆ = 0.10
S_align = LLM_Score(Abstracts_Top3, Venues, Model = "openai/gpt-oss-120b") ∈ [0.00, 1.00]

Evaluation Criteria: Evaluated via Groq's openai/gpt-oss-120b inference engine on the abstracts of the 3 most recent papers. The prompt explicitly audits:

  • Direct alignment with UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) & national development in Bangladesh (health, flood mitigation, agriculture, education).
  • Engagement with ethical AI, renewable energy, climate resilience, or frontier computing paradigms.
  • Methodological rigor and originality rather than generic review synthesis.
Capacity Classification

Standardized RCI Rank Tiers

Based on the final score [0.00 – 1.00], researchers are classified into five objective capacity tiers.

0.00 – 0.20

Emerging Scholar

Early-stage academic or graduate student. Best suited for peer co-authorship on exploratory projects under senior co-supervision.

Novice Tier
0.21 – 0.40

Developing Investigator

Active publishing cadence with a growing portfolio. Highly accessible mentor for undergraduate course projects and regional conferences.

Junior Tier
0.41 – 0.60

Established Researcher

Solid independent publication history in indexed venues with steady citation momentum. Dependable thesis co-supervisor or primary advisor.

Intermediate Tier
0.61 – 0.80

Senior Principal Scholar

Substantial international citations, high last-author leadership, and sustained funding. Ideal primary supervisor for Capstone and M.Sc. theses.

Advanced Tier
0.81 – 1.00

Distinguished Research Fellow

Global scholarly impact with high citation velocity and senior lab directorship. Pinnacle guidance for ambitious Q1 journal publications and Ph.D. pathways.
