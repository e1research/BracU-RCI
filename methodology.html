Research Capability Index (RCI): A Responsible Academic Assessment Framework

Moving beyond raw publication counts and journal prestige toward a multi-dimensional, transparent assessment of scientific contribution, authorship independence, and societal utility.

Initiative: Epoch One (E1) Research Group
Institution: BRAC University
Published: September 2026
Scale: 0.00 – 4.00 (CGPA Scale)

Executive Summary

Traditional researcher performance metrics—such as raw publication quantity, Journal Impact Factors (JIF), and cumulative citation indices (e.g., the h-index)—fail to capture demonstrated research capability. They reward hyper-prolific publication at the expense of substantive novelty, penalize junior researchers whose work has not had time to accrue legacy citations, and obscure individual authorship contribution. The Research Capability Index (RCI) is a transparent, reproducible, and multidimensional composite index designed for BRAC University researchers. RCI combines quantitative bibliometric velocity with deep qualitative AI appraisal grounded in the San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA), the Leiden Manifesto for Research Metrics, and the Hong Kong Principles for Fostering Research Integrity.

01. Motivation & Theoretical Grounding

For decades, university promotion committees and grant panels have relied on proxy indicators of quality—most notoriously, journal-level metrics like the Journal Impact Factor (JIF). As documented extensively by Seglen (1997) and Waltman (2016), citation distributions within any journal are heavily skewed: a tiny fraction of papers drive the majority of a journal's impact factor, rendering the journal's name an unscientific proxy for an individual article's quality.

To rectify systemic flaws in academic evaluation, the RCI framework operationalizes three foundational declarations of responsible research assessment:

DORA (2012)

Eliminates journal venue prestige as a surrogate for quality; assesses scientific value based on substantive content and methodological innovation.

Leiden Manifesto (2015)

Mandates quantitative metrics to support, not supplant, qualitative expert evaluation; demands transparency and account for field differences.

Hong Kong Principles (2020)

Rewards open research practices, rigorous replication, transparent reporting, and societal well-being over raw publication volume.

02. Dimensional Submetric Architecture

The RCI composite index aggregates seven orthogonal submetrics ($M_1$ through $M_7$), balancing quantitative bibliometrics with automated qualitative AI assessment:

Metric Evaluation Dimension Assessment Method Weight
M1 Peer-Reviewed Journal Output Sigmoid saturation of journal articles 15%
M2 First-Author Ratio Direct technical execution & intellectual independence 15%
M3 Last-Author Ratio Senior supervision, mentoring & lab leadership 10%
M4 Annualized Citation Velocity Annualized citations normalized over career span 10%
M5 Originality & Novelty LLM qualitative analysis of substantive problem framing 20%
M6 Human Usefulness & Societal Relevance LLM evaluation of real-world public/clinical utility 10%
M7 Technical Competency & Rigor LLM assessment of validation, data, & reproducibility 20%
Total Weight Sum 100%

03. Mathematical Formulations

M1: Peer-Reviewed Journal Output Weight: 15%

To prevent hyper-prolific publishing from artificially dominating the index (and because higher publication volume already correlates with higher citation velocity in $M_4$), $M_1$ applies a standard sigmoid activation function:

M_1 = \sigma(N) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-N}}

where $N$ is the total count of peer-reviewed journal articles (indexed under OpenAlex type: article).

M2: First-Author Ratio 15%

Captures lead execution, hands-on empirical work, and authorship independence (Tscharntke et al., 2007):

M_2 = \frac{N_{\text{first\_author}}}{N}

M3: Last-Author Ratio 10%

Captures senior supervision, intellectual steering, and principal investigator mentoring role:

M_3 = \frac{N_{\text{last\_author}}}{N}

M4: Annualized Citation Velocity Weight: 10%

Raw citation counts unfairly bias senior researchers over early-career scholars. $M_4$ first calculates the annualized citation rate over the active publication career span, then normalizes the unbounded rate to $[0, 1]$ using a soft-saturation curve ($k = 15$):

\text{CPY} = \frac{C_{\text{total}}}{\max(1, \; Y_{\text{recent}} - Y_{\text{oldest}})}
M_4 = \frac{\text{CPY}}{\text{CPY} + 15}

M5–M7: Qualitative Assessment via Calibrated AI Total Weight: 50%

Rather than assuming venue prestige dictates quality, the system passes abstracts and titles of the author's latest 5% papers ($\max(1, \lfloor 0.05 \times N \rfloor)$) to a large language model (Groq openai/gpt-oss-120b) prompted under DORA and Leiden guidelines.

M5: Novelty (20%)

Evaluates substantive conceptual framing, new algorithms, or novel problem formulations.

M6: Societal Impact (10%)

Evaluates practical utility, alignment with public well-being, sustainability, and real-world deployment.

M7: Technical Rigor (20%)

Evaluates experimental discipline, statistical validation, and reproducible methodology.

*Calibration Rule:* Solid academic papers are scored generously in the $0.70 - 1.00$ band; scores fall below $0.70$ only if the contribution is clearly incremental, derivative, or trivially minor.

04. The Excellence Incentive (Bonus Engine)

To reward researchers demonstrating mastery across individual dimensions, the RCI framework features a High-Performance Bonus Engine.

⭐ Bonus Rule: +0.05 per submetric where $M_i > 0.80$

1. First, calculate base normalized score: R_{\text{raw}} = \sum_{i=1}^7 w_i M_i 2. Compute bonus addition: \text{Bonus} = 0.05 \times \sum_{i=1}^7 \mathbf{1}(M_i > 0.80) 3. Apply bonus and cap at 1.0: R_{0 \dots 1} = \min\left(1.0, \; R_{\text{raw}} + \text{Bonus}\right) 4. Scale to university 4.00 CGPA standard: \mathbf{RCI} = 4.0 \times R_{0 \dots 1}

05. Data Integrity: The ORCID-First Pipeline

Automated academic indexing platforms (e.g., OpenAlex, Google Scholar) rely on statistical name-clustering algorithms that frequently fail on common naming patterns (e.g., "Md. Sabbir Ahmed"), conflating multiple individuals into a single bloated profile.

To guarantee 100% data integrity with zero namesake bloat, RCI implements an ORCID-first two-stage retrieval pipeline:

1. ORCID Public Registry Query (https://pub.orcid.org/v3.0/):
Fetches self-verified DOIs curated directly by the author.
2. OpenAlex DOI Query (https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=doi:...):
Queries OpenAlex strictly for those exact DOIs to extract citation velocity, abstracts, and authorships without relying on OpenAlex's internal author clustering.
